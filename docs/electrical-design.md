# Electrical design

The proposed power tree is 3S battery → rated PDB or soldered distribution harness → four independently routed ESC power pairs; a separate branch supplies a buck converter. Buck output is regulated 5 V to the verified DevKit input; its onboard 3.3 V rail supplies a compatible MPU breakout. A USB power bank independently powers the glove. Motor power and low-voltage power are separate physical paths with an appropriate common reference on the drone.

A separate buck is mandatory unless an identified ESC BEC is independently demonstrated to provide clean, adequate power. This design isolates all ESC BEC positive outputs and uses the buck. Select 5 V/2 A continuous as a provisional procurement requirement with documented 3S input including transients, current limiting and thermal derating. This is a conservative design allowance, not a verified regulator model. Measure both board peak loads and 3.3 V regulator temperature; consult [Espressif hardware guidelines](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32/schematic-checklist.html). Never feed 12.6 V to a 3V3/5V pin. Disconnect external 5 V before USB unless the actual board documents safe source isolation.

The battery connector serves as main isolation. A low-current panel switch is unsuitable for motor power. Add an accessible correctly rated removable connector; match polarity and anti-spark requirements to real current/capacitance. Do not specify a connector solely by a generic “XT60” label: obtain a genuine vendor continuous-current/temperature rating. Rating must exceed the derated measured branch total. A fuse is optional engineering work: choose interrupt rating and coordination against wiring and starting transients, not just nominal current.

Provisional wiring envelope: 60 A total, 15 A per motor, 0.3 m one-way main leads and 0.25 m one-way branches. Approximate copper resistance at room temperature: AWG12 ≈5.21 mΩ/m → a 0.6 m loop is **3.13 mΩ**, giving 0.188 V drop and 11.3 W at 60 A. AWG16 ≈13.17 mΩ/m → 6.59 mΩ for a 0.5 m loop, 0.099 V drop and 1.48 W at 15 A. These heat losses require thermal assessment; AWG12 main and AWG16 branches are **candidates**, not approved gauges. Insulation, bundling, airflow, connector resistance and higher actual current can require larger conductors. Do not size the pack path for “30 A” merely because each ESC is labelled 30 A. If 4×30 A is truly needed, redesign the whole path for ≥120 A and select a suitable pack.

Use twisted or adjacent signal/return pairs, separated from motor phases and high-current loops. Keep I2C short; check pullups with a scope. Use soldered/crimped power connections, heatshrink, strain relief, secured PCBs, insulated standoffs and guards as appropriate. Mount sensor near the center of mass with verified axis alignment and measured vibration response. Retain antenna clearance from carbon, conductors and the battery.

Add a low-ESR input capacitor near ESC distribution only after verifying ESC recommendations. Candidate 470–1000 µF, ≥25 V is not a finalized value; verify ripple current, inrush, polarity and scope-measured rail transients. Regulator input/output capacitors must follow its datasheet. Local sensor decoupling is required; confirm what is already on the breakout.

[Connection tables](connection-tables.md) govern provisional nets. [Power budget](power-budget.md) and [divider calculation](battery-analysis.md) quantify assumptions. Physical connector pinouts are blocked pending exact hardware identification.
